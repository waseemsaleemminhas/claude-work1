Also register **mahastarhealth.com** and **mahastarmedicalbilling.com** and redirect them to the main site. That protects the name, costs about $30–45/year, and leaves room to move up to a "Mahastar Health" master brand later.

*\*See the section 3 method notes. Final confirmation happens at registrar checkout.*

**Three findings that shaped the ranking:**
1. **A "Maha…" medical-billing company already exists:** Maha Medical Solutions (Alpharetta, GA) offers medical billing and RCM. That makes **"Mahastar Medical Solutions" the riskiest name** on the list.
2. **The "-star" billing space is crowded:** MedStar Health (a large health system with registered MEDSTAR marks), MedStar Billing Services, Star Billing Solutions, NorthStar RCM, Medical Billing Star and Stars Pro. Put the weight of the name on the distinctive "Mahastar" and avoid anything that reads as "M-something-Star Health/Billing".
3. **"MAHA" is a live political term in U.S. healthcare** ("Make America Healthy Again"). An HHS program, a CMS model and a pending trademark by MAHA TM LLC all use it. Always write the name as one word, **Mahastar**, never **MahaStar** or **MAHA Star**. Camel-casing invites the "MAHA + Star" reading.

### 2.2 Why the strong candidates work

**Mahastar RCM.** "RCM" is how practice administrators, CFOs and MSO leaders describe what they buy. Established brands in the category use it: R1 RCM, NorthStar RCM, and Savista at savistarcm.com. The short form reads as a *company*, while "…Billing Solutions" reads as a *shop*. It fits a logo, a LinkedIn banner and an email signature. The weakness is that a solo physician may not know the acronym. Fix that with a permanent descriptor: *"Mahastar RCM, Medical Billing & Revenue Cycle Management"*. Conflict risk is moderate-low. NorthStar RCM shares "-star RCM", but the distinctive first elements differ ("Maha-" vs "North").

**Mahastar Health.** This follows the dominant naming pattern among modern, well-funded RCM brands: *[coined word] + Health*, as in Ventra Health, Coronis Health, Plutus Health and AGS Health. It covers all 15 services and anything you add later, such as analytics, patient engagement or staffing. Its big weakness is **structural similarity to MEDSTAR HEALTH**. MedStar Health, Inc. is a large MD/DC/VA health system that owns several registered MEDSTAR marks dating back to the 1980s and appears in USPTO TTAB proceedings. "MAHASTAR HEALTH" and "MEDSTAR HEALTH" share initial M, the -STAR ending, the HEALTH suffix and the healthcare field. The likelihood-of-confusion test weighs exactly those factors: sight, sound and related goods/services. A trademark attorney should clear this name before you build on it.

**Mahastar Revenue Partners.** "Partners" is a trust word in outsourced healthcare services (Ensemble Health Partners). It tells a physician "we are an extension of your team", which counters the biggest objection to outsourcing billing: loss of control. "Revenue" is plain English that every practice owner understands, so it needs no acronym. Use **mahastarrevenue.com** as the primary domain because it is much shorter than mahastarrevenuepartners.com. Register both.

**Mahastar Practice Solutions.** This is the only strong name that *naturally* covers the non-billing half of the service list: virtual assistants, scheduling, data entry and back office. "Practice" tells private practices and small groups that the company is built for them, not for hospitals. It fits best if VA and back-office revenue will be a large share of the business. Its weakness is that "Solutions" is overused and slightly generic.

**Mahastar Medical Billing.** This is the easiest name to understand in a cold email subject line, and it matches the literal search term. Among the competitors reviewed, names containing "Billing" cluster at the **small-vendor end** of the market: Medical Billers and Coders, Star Billing Solutions, Medical Billing Star, MedStar Billing Services. Premium firms keep "billing" in the tagline, not the name. It also becomes a constraint once you sell VAs or credentialing to larger groups. Use it as a **secondary domain or landing page**, not the master brand.

### 2.3 Names to avoid and why

- **Mahastar Medical Solutions.** It is one word away from **Maha Medical Solutions**, an existing medical billing/RCM company in Alpharetta, GA. Same field, same buyers, and a near-identical structure: the highest confusion risk found.
- **Mahastar Healthcare / Health Partners / Health Group.** Same MedStar Health proximity issue as "Mahastar Health", without that name's brevity.
- **Mahastar Billing Solutions.** It overlaps with *Star Billing Solutions* (Phoenix, AZ medical billing), and "billing solutions" is the most generic small-vendor pattern.
- **Mahastar Medical Group.** Implies a physician practice (see section 1).

## 3. Domain research

### 3.1 Method

Commercial WHOIS/RDAP lookup sites were blocked from this research environment. Instead, each name was queried **directly against Verisign's authoritative .com/.net registry nameservers** (`a.gtld-servers.net`). A registered .com is delegated in the zone. An **NXDOMAIN** answer from the registry means the name has no delegation and is almost certainly unregistered.

- **Validation:** Known-taken controls came back TAKEN, as expected: `google.com`, `mahastar.com`, `northstarrcm.com`, `medstarbilling.com`, `starbillingsolutions.com`, `savistarcm.com`.
- **Caveat:** A domain can in rare cases be registered with no nameservers, or be on registry hold, and still return NXDOMAIN. Confirm at checkout with any registrar (Cloudflare Registrar, Namecheap, Porkbun, GoDaddy) **before announcing the brand**. Anyone can re-run the check with `research/check_domains.py`.

| Small-practice outsourcers | AnnexMed, Transcure, EZ MD Solutions, AIE Medical Management, Medical Billers and Coders, Star Billing Solutions, MedStar Billing Services, Medical Billing Star | Solo/small practices, clinics | Descriptive: **"…Billing", "…Solutions", "…Med"** |

### 4.2 What the successful players have in common

**Naming**
- **The more premium the firm, the less literal the name.** "Billing" almost never appears in the names of top-tier or well-funded mid-market firms. It appears in their *H1s and taglines*. Descriptive "billing" names concentrate among small outsourcers.
- **"[Distinctive word] + Health"** is the dominant modern pattern: Ventra, Coronis, Plutus, AGS. **"[Word] + RCM"** is the dominant pattern for revenue-cycle specialists: R1, NorthStar, Savista's domain.
- **"Partners"** is used to signal an extension-of-your-team relationship (Ensemble Health Partners).

**Positioning themes that recur**
1. **Outcomes, stated as metrics:** clean-claim rate, days in A/R, net collection rate, denial rate. Ventra organizes its message around "five core service metrics".
2. **Specialty depth:** "40+ specialties" (Transcure), or deep single-specialty lines (Ventra in anesthesia/radiology, Plutus in ASC/behavioral health/out-of-network).
3. **People + technology / AI:** CareCloud, CureMD and Plutus all lead with automation or AI claim scrubbing, backed by certified coders.
4. **Flexible, transparent pricing:** percentage of collections ("as low as 2.99%" for MedStar Billing Services), per-FTE or hourly (AnnexMed).
5. **Whole-practice support:** EZ MD Solutions bundles billing, virtual medical assistants, scribes and credentialing under one HIPAA-compliant umbrella. **This is closest to your service list and is a proven model for small practices.**

**Typical website structure**
`Home → Services (Billing, Coding, A/R & Denials, Credentialing, Eligibility/Prior Auth, Virtual Assistants) → Specialties → Who We Serve (Practices, Groups, Clinics, ASCs) → Why Us / Results (metrics, case studies) → Resources (blog, guides, calculators) → About (leadership, compliance: HIPAA, BAA, SOC 2) → CTA: "Free Revenue Audit / Free Practice Analysis"`.

**Implication for Mahastar:** Look like the mid-market tier (a clean distinctive name + Health/RCM, metrics, compliance badges) while selling a small-practice bundle like EZ MD's (billing + VA + credentialing). That combination is currently underserved: premium-looking firms tend to chase larger groups, and firms that serve small practices tend to look small.

**Credibility note:** U.S. practice owners often worry that an outsourced vendor is an offshore operation with weak HIPAA controls. Whatever name you choose, show a U.S. business address and phone number, a named U.S.-facing leadership team, BAA availability, HIPAA training and (eventually) a SOC 2 or HITRUST attestation prominently.

**Conclusion:** Use a **hybrid of B and A**: a corporate name that carries a descriptive tagline everywhere.
- Brand: **Mahastar RCM**
- Lockup / title tag / LinkedIn tagline: *"Medical Billing & Revenue Cycle Management for Physician Practices"*
- Put the SEO effort into **service × specialty pages** ("cardiology billing services", "prior authorization services for orthopedic practices"), not into the brand name.
- Use **mahastarmedicalbilling.com** for cold-email tracking links or a dedicated landing page, so descriptive clarity is available where it helps most.
